Epoxy Floor Coverage: How to Estimate Gallons for a 300 Square Foot Space

Estimating epoxy floor coverage sounds technical, but it comes down to one number: how many square feet one gallon covers at the thickness you want. Get that number right and the rest is division. This post shows the full calculation for a 300 square foot space and explains where people usually go wrong.

Start with the coverage rate

Manufacturers publish coverage in square feet per gallon at a given thickness, measured in mils, where one mil is one thousandth of an inch. A handy physical constant helps: one US gallon covers about 1,604 square feet at 1 mil. Divide that by your target thickness to get coverage per gallon.

Worked example: 15 ft by 20 ft, two coats at 10 mils each

• Area: 15 × 20 = 300 square feet

• Coverage at 10 mils: 1,604 ÷ 10 = 160.4 square feet per gallon

• One coat: 300 ÷ 160.4 = 1.87 gallons

• Two coats: 1.87 × 2 = 3.74 gallons

• Add 10 percent waste: 3.74 × 1.10 = 4.11 gallons

So you would buy around 4.2 gallons, which in practice means a 5 gallon kit if you prefer a margin, or one 3 gallon and one 1.5 gallon kit if you want to minimize leftovers.

Real coverage is lower than the theory

The 1,604 figure assumes a perfectly smooth, sealed surface and zero waste. Real concrete is porous, so the first coat often soaks in. That is why a primer or a thin first coat matters: it fills the pores so later coats stay on top. If your slab is rough or freshly poured, increase the estimate by 15 to 25 percent.

Thickness changes everything

Because coverage is inversely proportional to thickness, doubling the thickness halves the area one gallon covers. A 20 mil coat covers only 80.2 square feet per gallon, so the same 300 square feet needs 3.74 gallons in a single pass. Thicker coats can look glossier and hide flaws, but they cost more and can trap heat as they cure.

Planning the application

1. Divide the space into sections you can finish within the pot life

2. Mix only what you can spread in that window

3. Keep a wet edge between sections so seams do not show

4. Keep the room between 60 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit

5. Recoat within the window given on the data sheet

A short checklist

• Measure and multiply for area

• Pick thickness per coat in mils

• Divide 1,604 by mils to get coverage

• Multiply by number of coats

• Add waste and round up to the next kit size

Following this order every time keeps your estimate honest and your budget predictable.
